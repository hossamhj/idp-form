This is a proposal, not part of the programme. Nothing here is built or agreed. It exists because a real case arrived that none of the four templates covers, and because that case is about to repeat. Read it, argue with it, then decide.
A path we do not have
Someone joins as GM before their community opens
They are not new to work — they are new to our work. They hold no functions yet, so there is nothing to hand over and nothing to release. Template 4 does not describe them, and neither does onboarding. Today they shadow whoever is available, and we find out whether it worked on opening day.

Why the existing template does not fit
Template 4 takes an AGM to GM. Its first half is entirely about giving work away — which assumes the person already holds it.

Put them through T4 and the first two quarters are meaningless — a handover map for functions they do not have. The gap is not a few functions. It is the whole operating model.
This is not an exception
It is the expansion pipeline.
Five communities in 2027 means five of these.
Without a method, that is five different improvisations — each one depending on which GM happened to be free, and on how good that GM happens to be at explaining what they do.

The cost of not deciding is not visible until an opening goes badly, and by then it is a building, not a training issue.
What they actually need to understand
Not invented — read from ops_function_operating. These are the 27 functions a GM touches, grouped by focus area. The shadowing is organised around these four blocks, not around weeks.
Rotate across more than one GM. Someone who shadows a single manager copies their habits — including the ones we would not choose to spread. One block, one GM, chosen for strength in that block.
How we would know it worked
One question, tested by real work.
Can they open the building without asking?
Tested by running something real in an existing community — a full cycle, with the resident GM reviewing the outcome only — before they are handed a new one.

Not a certificate of attendance. Not a conversation. A month of a building running under them, somewhere the cost of a mistake is recoverable.
Three options
The first two are cheap. The third is a decision.
1Record them, and leave shadowing outside the system
They appear as GM · practising. The system says nothing about how they are prepared. Honest, costs nothing, and repeats the improvisation five times.
2Two fields on their record
Who they shadow and until when. Enough for a manager to see it exists. No content, no test, no way to tell whether it worked.
3An Incoming GM path Recommended
The 27 functions as the map · four blocks · a named GM per block, chosen for strength · and one test at the end that a building has to pass.

Why this one, when I argued against building a path for ordinary onboarding: onboarding is a person learning their own job, and it has one owner and a natural end. This is a building being handed over. It happens five times next year, the cost of getting it wrong is a community, and nobody currently owns the method.
The question underneath
Worth settling before the method, because it changes what the method is for.
Should the next GM come from outside, or be an AGM who crossed T4?
The programme is designed for the second. Today's reality is the first.

Five communities are five chances to promote from within — or five more times we hire from outside and explain the operating model from zero.

This is also what makes the proposed measure “≥80% of leadership vacancies filled from within” real rather than decorative: an outside GM hire counts against it. If we are not willing to have it count, we should not adopt the measure.
What is needed to go further
None of this can be built on assumptions.
Which community, and when
The handover date sets the whole schedule backwards from it.
Who can host each block
Four GMs, or fewer — and whether they have the hours.
Where the real test runs
Which existing community absorbs a month under a new manager.
A decision on the question above
Outside hire or internal promotion — as a stated preference, not a case-by-case habit.
