Who assesses, who decides, who audits — and why full visibility is safe once decision rights are separated.
Career Development ProgrammeSeptember 2026
The idea the whole model rests on

Sight without vote.

The People Team sees every record in the programme and decides none of them. That combination is what makes total transparency safe — and it is the reason nothing has to be hidden.
If the People Team could decide promotions
Full visibility would be dangerous. People would manage what they write, and the record would stop being true.
Because the People Team only audits
Full visibility is an asset. Talent planning works, bias becomes detectable, and appeals can be reviewed on substance.
01 · The principleFlow
The structure

Three pillars — no access restrictions among them

PILLAR ONE
Separated decision rights
The coach recommends. The sponsor decides. The People Team audits.

Nobody holds two of the three.
PILLAR TWO
Governed use
Every field has a stated purpose and a stated prohibition.

What is controlled is use, not access — and use is auditable.
PILLAR THREE
Full transparency
Everyone sees their own record, the criteria in advance, and who has access.

The harm comes from surprise, not from access.

An earlier draft protected the reflective parts of the record by hiding them from the People Team. That was the wrong instrument — it would have blinded talent planning and made bias impossible to detect, while solving a problem that use-governance solves better.

Why the coach cannot decide
Daily closeness is what makes their observation valuable — and exactly why it should not be final. The recommendation is written, reasoned, and carries real weight.
Why the sponsor cannot ignore it
A sponsor may overrule the coach — with a written reason recorded against the decision. And a promotion with no gate record is not something the system accepts.

05 · Governed useFlow
Why the reflective record needs protecting at all
A real entry
"Ran the evening handover on my own for the first time.
Forgot to flag the third-floor AC report — a colleague picked it up two hours later.
Next time: review open reports before the handover, not during it."

That is an admission of error — and it is the most valuable line in the system.

The risk was never that someone reads it. The risk is that it gets quoted in a promotion decision — at which point people write for the audience and the log dies within a month.

So the protection sits on use, not access. Everyone can read it. Nobody may cite it against you.

06 · ProtectionFlow
Pillar three · transparency

Three guarantees to the person being developed

One
No hidden file
Every assessment and note written about someone is visible to them.

If they are not entitled to read it, it is not fit to be written.
Two
Criteria before assessment
Nobody is measured against a standard they have not seen. A criterion changed mid-programme does not apply to anyone already in it.
Three
Access is visible
Everyone can see who has sight of their record.

The harm comes from surprise, not from access.
One sequencing rule enforced by the system rather than by good intentions: the self-assessment locks before the manager's rating becomes visible. The gap between them is the substance of the monthly conversation — and it means nothing if either was written with sight of the other.
07 · TransparencyFlow
The rules

Eight, grouped by the pillar they serve

SEPARATED RIGHTS
1
The level is granted two levels above. The line manager recommends only.
2
The People Team sees everything and decides nothing.
3
No promotion without a complete gate record.
GOVERNED USE
4
The practice log and the self/manager gap are never cited in an evaluative decision.
5
Assessments are superseded, never deleted — the record keeps both, and who changed it.
TRANSPARENCY
6
The employee sees everything written about them.
7
No assessment against a criterion published after the fact.
8
Quarterly calibration is mandatory — every coach rates the same case, and the spread is discussed.

Rule eight is the one most often skipped and the one that fails most quietly. Without it, "Consistent" means something different in each property within a year — and a level stops being comparable across the company.

08 · The rulesFlow
When someone disagrees

The People Team now reviews the substance as well as the process

With full visibility, an appeal is no longer a paperwork check.
On an assessment
Raised with the sponsor, who reviews the substance and may ask the coach to reconsider.
On a gate decision
The People Team reviews both:
Process — were criteria published in advance, were sessions held, was the recommendation reasoned?
Substance — was the same standard applied to others at this level, in other properties?

If a flaw is found, the decision returns to the sponsor. The People Team does not substitute its own judgement — doing so would make it a decision-maker, which is exactly what rule two prevents. Full sight, no vote.

09 · AppealFlow
The governance risk specific to us

Growth, not favouritism, is what will bend the standard

Promotion here means taking a new building, not replacing the person who trained you. That removes the usual conflict of interest — a coach whose people get promoted has expanded the company, and their incentive points the right way.
The real risk
A building opens in four months and no AGM is ready.
Pressure to fill the seat → the bar moves → the promotion fails.
The gate is not negotiable for expansion
Programme length sits inside the expansion plan
The People Team watches for the standard slipping
12-month retention catches it afterwards

A gate pass rate that rises during rapid expansion is a warning, not good news. This is exactly the kind of pattern full visibility lets the People Team see early.
