Career Development
Programme

A 12-month development cycle for each team member, built on the Operating Playbook.
Proposal to leadershipSeptember 2026
What it is

A 12-month cycle per person, ending in promotion or recognised mastery

Each quarter has one goal and three functions to learn. The person keeps a practice log; the manager coaches and assesses.
Q1
Understand the work
Observe. Shadow every touchpoint. No functions assigned yet.
Q2
Deliver on my own
Three low-risk functions. First time working without supervision.
Q3
Handle the unfamiliar
Contractors, first incident, first real ownership.
Q4
Own the outcome
A full quarter holding real scope. Then the promotion gate.
Assessed on
Craft — does the work to standard
Conduct — how they work with residents and the team
Scope — can hold responsibility without supervision
01 · What it isFlow
What actually happens

Three meetings. That is the whole operating rhythm.

WhenLengthWithWhat happens in it
Weekly15 minThe person who owns that functionWatch one task, give one correction. Nothing else.
Monthly45 minLine managerRead the practice log. Compare self-rating to manager rating. Agree one commitment.
Quarterly90 minManager + GMClose the quarter goal. Choose the next three functions.
Manager time
4–5 hours per month per person in the programme. Most teaching is done by whoever owns the function — usually a peer, not the manager.
Why the weekly is only 15 minutes
Long development sessions get cancelled in a busy week. Short ones survive.
02 · How it runsFlow
Where the content comes from

We do not write competencies. We read them from the Playbook.

The operating model already records what each role does on each of 48 functions — assist, execute, own or oversee. The difference between two roles is the development plan.

Worked example · the twelve months

Nine functions taught deliberately. Three per quarter.

The other 17 convert through normal exposure and are checked at the gate. Only genuinely new work gets a teacher and a log.

08 · The gateFlow
What we get

Six outcomes we hold the programme to

Succession cover
Someone ready for every critical role, instead of an external search each time one opens.
Buildings that do not depend on individuals
A function still works when its owner is away. That is what "own" means in this model.
Promotions that hold
We promote on readiness for the next role, not on performance in the current one.
Faster time to standard
A defined learning path for new joiners instead of learning by osmosis.
One standard across buildings
A Senior in Narjs East means the same as a Senior in Granada. Quarterly calibration keeps it that way.
Retention
People who can see a path stay. People who cannot, look for one elsewhere.
09 · OutcomesFlow
What we get

These are operational problems, not HR problems

What happens without it
A building slips because one person was away
An external hire for a role we could have filled
A strong performer promoted into a role they fail in
A capable person leaves for a clearer path
The standard drifting between buildings
Which is why
Each of these shows up in building quality, resident satisfaction and cost — not in an HR report.

That is the reason the programme sits inside the operating system rather than beside it.
10 · OutcomesFlow
How it fits what we already run

Three connections to the operating system

One
It reads the Playbook
Development content comes from the same function definitions the staffing model and task engine use. Update the model and every plan updates with it.
Two
Evidence comes from real work
Completed tasks are already recorded against the function. We read them rather than asking a manager to write a summary.
Three
It unblocks lateral cover
When a role is absent, work currently rises to the level above. Covering sideways instead needs a record of what people can do — which is what this produces.
One thing is missing from the operating model itself: developing people is not a function. It appears in every job description and in none of the 48 functions — so it has no allocated hours, no standard, and nobody accountable for it.
11 · IntegrationFlow
Who is in it, and what it costs

Four to six people at a time — not everyone

To enter, all four must be true
Has mastered the current role — measurable from the model
Has done each function enough times to be judged
Has worked a full operating year — renewal season, summer peak, Ramadan
No open performance concern
Staying at level is a legitimate outcome. Someone who does not want promotion grows through depth, teaching others, and improving the standard itself.
Cost per person
4–5
manager hours per month
Low because most teaching is done by whoever owns the function — a peer, not the manager.
Rollout
Start with one or two people in one building for a full quarter, then widen one building at a time.

13 · MeasurementFlow
What we need to proceed

Four decisions

One
Record each person's operational role
Nothing in the programme resolves without it. Data entry in Team Management — and the Playbook pilot needs it anyway.
Two
Add developing people as a function
So it gets hours in the staffing model, a standard, and an accountable owner — like any other work.
Three
Commit the manager time
4–5 hours per month per person, in the calendar rather than in principle.
Four
Approve real scope for the Q4 assignment
The final gate requires holding something that matters, with the manager stepping back.

On approval: one pilot person, one building, one quarter — reviewed before we widen.
