The governing principle

Separation of powers

Any development system that combines assessment, decision and oversight in one pair of hands becomes either favouritism or fear.

No one holds two of the three. The coach who works with someone every day cannot also be the one who promotes them. The person who promotes cannot also be the one who audits whether the process was fair.

02 · The rolesFlow
The employee

Two rights that are not negotiable

Right one
No hidden file
Every assessment and every note written about someone is visible to them.

If they are not entitled to read it, it is not fit to be written.
Right two
Criteria published before they apply
Nobody is assessed against a standard they have not seen.

A criterion changed mid-programme does not apply to anyone already in it.
One sequencing rule enforced by the system, not by good intentions: the self-assessment locks before the manager's rating becomes visible. The gap between the two is the substance of the conversation — and it only means anything if neither was written with sight of the other.
03 · The employeeFlow
The coach and the sponsor

The coach recommends. The sponsor decides.

Why the coach cannot decide
They work with the person daily. That closeness is exactly what makes their observation valuable — and exactly why it should not be the final word.

The recommendation is written and reasoned, and it carries weight.
Why the sponsor cannot ignore it
The sponsor may overrule the coach — with a written reason recorded against the decision.

And a promotion without a complete gate record is not something the system will accept.

Neither constraint is administrative. Both exist because a promotion decision made by one person, with no record, is indistinguishable from a favour.

04 · Coach and sponsorFlow
HR — the distinction that matters most

HR measures the process,
never the person.

HR asks
  • Was the monthly session held?
  • Were the criteria published in advance?
  • Did calibration happen this quarter?
  • Is one coach's outcome pattern unusual?
HR does not ask
  • Is this person ready?
  • Was that rating fair?
  • Which of the two is better?

The moment HR becomes a party to assessing individuals, it stops being the guardian of the process and becomes an opponent — and people stop being honest in front of it.

06 · VisibilityFlow
The one protection people question

Why the practice log is not open to everyone

A real entry
"Ran the evening handover on my own for the first time.
Forgot to flag the third-floor AC report — a colleague picked it up two hours later.
Next time: review open reports before the handover, not during it."

That is an admission of error. If everyone can read it, it gets written for the audience — and stops being useful within a month.

The employee and their coach see it. The sponsor sees a summary. HR sees that it was written, not what it says.

The same reasoning protects the self-assessment: make the gap a metric on individuals and people will rate themselves strategically rather than honestly.

07 · ProtectionFlow
The rules

Eight, and they are short on purpose

1
The level is granted two levels above. The line manager recommends only.
2
No assessment against a criterion that was not published beforehand.
3
Assessments are never deleted, only superseded — the record keeps both, and who changed it.
4
The self-assessment locks before the manager's rating is visible.
5
HR measures the process, not people.
6
No promotion without a complete gate record.
7
Quarterly calibration is mandatory — every coach rates the same case, and the spread is discussed.
8
The employee sees everything written about them.
08 · The rulesFlow
When someone disagrees

The appeal reviews the process, not the judgement

On an assessment
Raised with the sponsor, who reviews the substance.
On a gate decision
HR reviews four things only:
· Were the criteria published in advance?
· Were the sessions actually held?
· Was the recommendation reasoned?
· Was the same standard applied to others?

If a flaw is found, the decision returns to the sponsor. HR does not substitute its own judgement — doing so would make it a decision-maker, which is precisely what rule five prevents.

09 · AppealFlow
The governance risk specific to us

Growth, not favouritism, is what will bend the standard

Promotion here means taking a new building, not replacing the person who trained you. That removes the usual conflict of interest — a coach whose people get promoted has expanded the company, and their incentive is aligned.
The real risk
A building opens in four months and no AGM is ready.
Pressure to fill the seat → the bar moves → the promotion fails.
The gate is not negotiable for expansion
Programme length sits inside the expansion plan
HR watches for the standard slipping
12-month retention catches it after the fact

A gate pass rate that rises during rapid expansion is a warning, not good news.

10 · The real riskFlow
In one line

The coach observes. The sponsor decides.
HR makes sure it was done properly.
And the person sees all of it.

None of these constraints slow the programme down. They are what make a promotion decision defensible a year later — to the person who received it, and to the person who did not.
